Eat the layers

The Arrival City walk, rebuilt as a snack crawl. Same thesis, different evidence: every migration that built this stretch of the city left a bakery, and you can eat your way through them in order, Chinatown to Ossington, one pastry per diaspora.

Distance
About 3 km, flat, and the pace is set by digestion rather than legs. Two to three hours.
Getting there
Line 2 east to Spadina, then the 510 streetcar south to the St Andrew St stop. About 30 to 35 minutes door to door.
Getting home
From the Ossington strip: 63 bus north to Ossington station, or walk up to Bloor. Two stops home on Line 2.
Bring cash
Mashion leans cash-only and Golden Patty is safest in cash too. Forty dollars covers the entire walk with change.
Ends at
Third-wave espresso, or a brewery patio, on Ossington. Your choice of final layer.

The idea

Sister walk to The Arrival City. That one starts on the erased neighbourhood and carries the history; this one starts with an egg tart and lets the food make the same argument.

The claim is simple: each wave of arrival left the city a baked good, and along one 3 km line you can eat them in roughly the order they arrived. A Hong Kong egg tart on Spadina, where Chinatown rebuilt after the city demolished its first one. A Jamaican patty and a Mexican churro in Kensington, the layers that arrived after 1962 and 1980. A pastel de nata on Dundas West, where the Azorean Portuguese moved after 1953. And a flat white on Ossington, which is the newest arrival of all, and the one with the most money.

Chinatown's own bakery history makes the point before you take a bite. The two most-loved bakeries of the last generation are both gone: Ding Dong Pastries on Spadina closed in 2022, and Furama, thirty years at Spadina and Dundas, died in September 2020. The one you are starting at is the survivor. Eat accordingly.

The walk

Stop one

Mashion Bakery

345 Spadina Ave · the Chinese layerMapStreet view

Open since 2011, daily 9 to 9, and famous for prices that ignore the decade: buns that opened at three for a dollar and still hover around fifty cents, egg tarts about the same. Get one pastry-crust egg tart, one pineapple bun, and something savoury for ballast. This is the working Chinatown bakery, queue of locals and all, standing in for the two famous ones that didn't survive.

More

Before you leave Spadina, look north a block: 315 to 325 Spadina is the development block that triggered the 2019 fight for Chinatown's future, and the plan that answers it passed council three weeks ago. The full story is on the Arrival City page; here it is just the view with your tart.

Cash. Card acceptance is inconsistent and the prices barely justify the terminal fee. There is an ATM down the block.
Stop two

Into the Market, past the Minsker

10 St Andrew St · the Jewish layerMapStreet view
200 m3 min

Enter Kensington along St Andrew Street, the way the Jewish community did from 1905, and pass Anshei Minsk, the 1930 Byzantine Revival synagogue that served the Spadina garment trade. The layer that founded the Market did not leave a bakery on this route; it left the Market itself, and the shop-grafted-onto-a-house architecture every later arrival baked inside.

Stop three

Golden Patty

187 Baldwin St · the Caribbean layerMapStreet view
250 m3 min

From Devni's own saved list. A beef patty here is the 1962-onwards layer in pastry form: the Immigration Act changed, Jamaican shopkeepers arrived in the Market, and the patty became as native to Toronto as the bagel. Open daily from morning until early evening, and one patty each is correct pacing, because there are two stops left.

Stop four

Pancho's Bakery, then the Augusta strip

Augusta Ave · the Latin American layerMapStreet view
200 m3 min

Churros, made fresh all day, three for about five dollars, from the husband-and-wife bakery that has anchored the Market's Mexican presence for years. This is the 1980s layer: Chilean and Central American refugees first, Mexican bakers after, the empanada shops in between.

More

Walk the length of Augusta while the churro lasts: Perola's at 247, founded Portuguese and now the Latin American anchor, is the whole neighbourhood's succession story inside one shop, and Casa Acoreana at Baldwin and Augusta is the Portuguese-era nut-and-coffee counter still trading. The honest counterweights are here too: the cannabis shops, and 27 to 29 Kensington Ave, which the community land trust bought to hold the line. The full argument is on the Arrival City page; today you are just eating in it.

Pancho's has two Kensington addresses in circulation, 214 Augusta and 235 Augusta, and their own pages disagree. It is one short block; follow the churro smell and trust the awning, not the internet.
Stop five

The nata duel on Dundas West

Caldense at 1209, Nova Era at 1172 · the Portuguese layerMapStreet view
1.3 km west along Dundas17 min, which is the digestion leg

Leave the Market westward and walk Dundas West into Little Portugal, which exists because the Azorean families who arrived after the 1953 Canada-Portugal labour agreement moved through Kensington and kept going west. The two bakeries that anchor the strip face off a block apart: Caldense at 1209, the traditionalist, and Nova Era at 1172, which started here on Dundas and grew into a small empire, open five in the morning to eleven at night.

More

The move: buy one pastel de nata at each, eat them side by side, and declare a winner. The custard should still be warm somewhere in the day's rotation, the top properly scorched. This is the same pastry the Portuguese layer painted onto Kensington's houses, in edible form: a tradition carried whole across an ocean and maintained out of pride.

Stop six

Ossington, the newest layer

299 Ossington Ave, or 124 Ossington · pick your endingMapStreet view
500 m7 min

Turn down Ossington and finish inside the most recent migration of all, which is capital. Two endings:

More

Misc Coffee, 299 Ossington, for the espresso version: opened June 2025, owner placed second in Canada's Brewers Cup, beans from a Montreal competition roaster. The flat white here is as much an artefact of its arrival wave as the egg tart was of its own; the difference is what each wave paid in rent. Or Bellwoods Brewery, 124 Ossington, for the patio version, which on a warm August afternoon is the best beer garden seat in the west end.

The closing thought, carried over from the sister walk: Ossington was Portuguese sports bars and karaoke rooms within living memory. Every layer you ate today was once the new arrival that made the old-timers nervous, and the strip you are ending on is simply the latest one, priced accordingly.

What is verified and what to check on the day

Verified open in 2026: Mashion (daily 9 to 9), Nova Era (daily 5 to 23), Perola's, Misc Coffee, Bellwoods. Verified recently but check hours: Golden Patty (daily, sources split between 8 and 9 open, 7pm close), Pancho's (daily, address confusion noted above), Caldense (open per 2026 listings, exact hours unpublished).

Verified dead, so do not detour to them: Ding Dong Pastries (2022) and Furama (2020) in Chinatown, and Jumbo Empanadas in Kensington announced its closure in February 2026, owner retiring, so treat any open shutter there as a bonus rather than a plan.

Kensington is quietest before noon and the bakeries are freshest in the morning. A 10:30 start eats best.
