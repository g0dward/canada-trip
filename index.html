Day by day

Monday: the river, then the lake

The Oxtongue in the morning, Lake of Bays and the tower as it clearsMap

Yesterday's 40mm is the opportunity. Late August normally means low water and modest falls; today the Oxtongue will be running properly. Morning on the river: Oxtongue Rapids Park (free, no permit, a trail along the water and rock you can climb down to) and Ragged Falls 25 minutes out, a short trail with a steep spur to the base of a 25 m drop. Afternoon on the lake: south to Dorset for the Scenic Lookout Tower at about 16:30 as the cloud breaks, 142 m above Lake of Bays looking over the water and the western edge of Algonquin, last entry 17:00, $11 a car. Dwight Beach if you want to stand on the lake, and the Trading Bay patio is on the water at Dorset.

Food is a waypoint, not the point: Henrietta's Pine Bakery and the Dwight Market butcher counter are ten-minute stops as you pass through Dwight, and Robinson's General Store is worth twenty minutes in Dorset if you are there anyway.

After 40mm, two things. The spur down to the base of Ragged Falls will be slick, so proper shoes. And Ragged Falls' car park needs a day-use vehicle permit bought online (QR code on the sign); Oxtongue Rapids is free.

Dinner: The Portuguese House is closed Mondays. Either cook the butcher haul, or That Little Place by the Lights (handmade pasta, no reservations, arrive by 6) is open Monday to Saturday.

If it pours: swap the tower for the Lake of Bays Marine Museum by the Dorset docks (ring ahead), or cut Dorset entirely and make it Dwight, the butcher, and a Canvas afternoon.

Tuesday: Limberlost

Solitaire plus Echo Rock, the 3.5 to 4 hour classic. Dry and breezy is its weatherMap

Dry with gusts to the mid-30s: forest walking weather, since the trees take the wind. Free, no cap, waiver over breakfast (needs the RAV4 plate), gates 9 to 5, enforced. The route: Solitaire plus the Echo Rock spur, 10 to 12 km, 3.5 to 4 hours, the best single viewpoint on the western side. Feeling strong, swap Echo Rock for Buck Lake plus the short steep Buck Lake Lookout, about 12 km with a summit sting.

Evening: the last guided Group of Seven mural tour of your stay runs at 6pm from the Tom Thomson statue, $15. Dinner: Portuguese House is closed Tuesdays too; That Little Place or the Brewhouse.

If the forecast betrays: light rain under trees is fine; a real washout means swapping in Thursday's town plan and pushing Limberlost to Thursday.

Walks

Ranked by what they give you rather than by distance. The short answer: Limberlost is the walk of the week, Hidden Valley is the one you can do on twenty minutes’ notice.

The standout

Limberlost Forest and Wildlife Reserve

1002 Buck Lake Landing, 30 minMap

Ten thousand acres, seventy kilometres of trail, twenty lakes, free, and no marketing budget. The only place here that gives you a genuine half day on the hill rather than a car park and a viewpoint. Reviewers keep describing it as what you do after you see the queue at Arrowhead.

The one to do is Solitaire plus Buck Lake plus the Buck Lake Lookout, about 12 km over four hours, two contrasting lakes and a short steep sting at the end that gives the day a summit. For quieter still, start at the Barlow lot and take the Kalonga wetland boardwalks.

Free, no bookingSolitaire loop 6.3 kmRealistically 150 to 200 m of climb
Two catches. A safety waiver is mandatory, has to be done online before you arrive, and asks for the vehicle registration. And the 09:00 to 17:00 gate hours are enforced, with vehicles ticketed or removed, so no dawn starts. Ignore the old master trail guide still linked from their own site, it lists Solitaire at 13 km when it is 6.3.

Arrowhead

10 minutes north of HuntsvilleMap

You have done Stubb's Falls. The only route here that makes a proper morning is Arrowhead Lake plus Beaver Meadow, just under nine kilometres, which Ontario Parks themselves recommend as a pair. Round the lake through wetland edges where herons work the shallows, then over Porcupine Bluffs past a beaver pond. Everything else is a half-hour stroll. Big Bend Lookout is a genuinely good view over a gooseneck in the Big East River but it is fifty metres from the car park, so treat it as a stop. Skip Homesteaders, it is half ATV track.

Ragged Falls on foot, and Lions Lookout

Ragged Falls is worth three quarters of an hour and not a day: under a kilometre of trail with a steep rough spur to the base. Bolt it onto an Algonquin day, since a corridor permit also covers its car park, or reach it by water. Lions Lookout in Huntsville is not a hike at all, there is a paved road to the top, but the view over Fairy Lake and the town is genuinely good, so drive up in the evening with a beer.

There is no Buttermilk Falls near Dwight. The one people mean is in Haliburton County, an hour and ten each way, for a two-minute walk. Only worth it if you are already going to the Dorset tower.

Water

The second paddle: Oxtongue River to Ragged Falls

Corrected: this was written as the fixed point and as the otter paddle, and it is neither. The otters were in the North Arm of Lake Opeongo, confirmed from the photo's own coordinates, and that trip is booked for Wednesday. This run is the good second paddle, needing no booking, for any calm morning. Three to four hours return, out across Oxtongue Lake then up the river, landing at the base of the falls where you can scramble the rock and swim. Late August low water means the falls are less dramatic than in spring but far more of the rock is open. 20 to 25 minutes from the door.

There is nothing to reserve, and that is the thing to plan around. It is first come first served on a finite fleet of boats, so the day is won or lost by being there when the doors open. Three things follow from that:

Ask for the "Ragged Falls Paddling Experience" by name. The named day-trip rate is cheaper than the generic same-day rate for the identical boat: $49.98 for a tandem canoe against $53.98, or $46.98 for a single kayak. Paddles, buoyancy aids and safety kits are all included. About $56 with tax.

Pick a weekday and go at opening. The falls are quiet first thing and busy by midday, when the separate Highway 60 car park crowd arrives on foot. Oxtongue Lake also picks up wind through the afternoon, and the lake leg is the exposed part. Avoid Saturday the 23rd and Friday the 28th.

No park permit needed. The store is outside Algonquin, and arriving at the falls by water means you never touch the pay-and-display car park. This is the one good day up here that costs nothing but the boat.

Two unknowns, and no phone this trip

Algonquin Outfitters' Oxtongue Lake opening hour is still unconfirmed: sources split between 8am and 9am, and that hour decides whether you have the falls to yourselves. Ask at the counter on Wednesday, since you are at their Opeongo store anyway.

Same for the tie-downs. The RAV4 has no roof racks, so whether a same-day boat can go off-site decides whether the Lower Oxtongue paddle below is available at all. Both are counter questions now rather than phone calls.

The find: the Lower Oxtongue to Marsh's Falls

Launch from Dwight Beach, paddle a kilometre west along the bay to the river mouth, then four and a half kilometres up a meandering wetland river to Marsh's Falls. 8.8 km return, about three hours at an exploring pace, free, no permit, no gate. Dwight is 15 to 18 minutes from the door and on the way to Oxtongue Lake.

This is a wildlife corridor rather than a scenic lake, which is the difference. Herons, beaver, otter, turtles and moose are all on the expected list, and the river runs through the low wet outwash ground that moose actually use. The Lake of Bays Heritage Foundation runs an annual eco-tour on exactly this route and publishes a free downloadable paddle guide, so get that before you fly.

One catch, worth a phone call. There is no rental at Dwight Beach, and the RAV4 will not have roof racks. Ask Algonquin Outfitters directly whether a same-day rental can go off-site and whether they will hire you tie-downs. If yes, this is the best paddle of the week.

No driving at all

The canal outside the door

Zero minutes

The historic Portage Canal joins Fairy Lake to Peninsula Lake and is navigable. At midday it is a working powerboat route through a resort town and not worth paddling. At 06:30 it is glass. Worth doing once for the novelty of stepping out of the door onto water.

Skip Canoe Lake, and the one that got away

Canoe Lake is the most famous launch in Algonquin and also the busiest. It is an access point, an outfitter, a restaurant and a permit office on one bay. For someone who has been to Algonquin twice, the Tom Thomson pilgrimage is a queue with a cairn at the end. Smoke Lake is 600 metres further west on the same road and is a better paddle. Skip Cache Lake and Tea Lake entirely.

The Big East River is the best paddle you probably cannot do. A 17 km run past the Arrowhead side, sandy-bottomed and quiet, but it is one way and needs two cars. With one RAV4 and no documented shuttle service, it does not work. Worth ten minutes ringing a Huntsville taxi, because if one will run the shuttle this beats everything except the Lower Oxtongue.

The steam train is worth ten dollars, Tuesday to Saturday, tickets on the day only. Skip the pioneer village attached to it, and skip Santa's Village entirely, which is a fifty dollar a head toddler theme park.

The SS Bigwin is not sailing at all in 2026. The 1910 steamship that runs from Dwight Beach, ten minutes from your door, is ashore for repairs for the whole season. Third-party listings are still advertising it and the Cruise and Dine to Port Cunnington. Both are off.

On the day, verified

Park permits: book at 7am with coffee

reservations.ontarioparks.ca · 1-888-668-7275 from 7am

Daily vehicle permits ($21, one per vehicle, covers everyone in it) are sold from five days ahead down to the day itself, online or by phone from 7am. They are non-refundable with no date changes, which is exactly why waiting for the weather is correct. Late-August weekdays carry low sell-out risk at both Algonquin's Hwy 60 corridor and Arrowhead; capacity problems are a weekend and fall-colours phenomenon. Enter the RAV4's plate, pre-register, and drive straight in past the gate queue. For Algonquin, select "Hwy 60 Corridor" specifically, and that day's permit also covers Ragged Falls parking.

The one exception: a weekend Arrowhead visit. Arrowhead genuinely fills on summer weekends and turns away cars without permits. If a Saturday or Sunday visit appeals, book it the moment the plan firms up.

Limberlost waiver

limberlostforest.com, free

Mandatory online safety waiver, no capacity cap, fill it the same morning: names, contacts, emergency contact, the car's plate. Gates 9 to 5, enforced, cars that skip the waiver get ticketed.

Marsh's Falls registration

Lake of Bays Heritage Foundation

Mandatory per visit, and the car park location arrives by email only after registering. How fast that email lands is unverified, so register the evening before rather than the morning of.

Sunday 23 August

Storm day, and it earned the name

Correction, 16:00: the morning entry here said the washout had shrunk overnight and that the day was worth a dry-ish morning walk. That was wrong, and it came from trusting a model blend over the Canadian model and the live station. Environment Canada has since issued a rainfall statement: another 20 to 40mm, multiple rounds of showers and thunderstorms, through the afternoon and possibly the evening, with pooling on roads and washouts near creeks and culverts. Gaps in the rain today are lulls between rounds, not the clear.

Both models hold total cloud all evening, so the golden hour is off: sunset at 20:10 will be flat grey. Nothing today costs anything. Three clocks: the LCBO closed at 18:00 (Sundays), Bullock's at 20:00, and Metro at 22:00, which makes Metro the unhurried option tonight. It is the top-up shop rather than the big one, so the proper shop is Bullock's at 07:00 tomorrow, on the corner of Hwy 60 and Hwy 11 and directly on the way east. Keep away from creeks and low ground.

Update, 18:20: the rain has stopped, the wind has fallen from 24 to 11 km/h, and GEM shows nothing further tonight, though the rainfall statement technically still stands. So the evening is walkable after all: shop first, then the Hidden Valley Lookout Trail, which is high ground and clear of the flooding. Park at 45.34765, -79.13262; it is 888 m each way and 43 m of climb. Torches for the walk back, dark at 20:41.

The lesson, now written into the notes: live station observations and the Canadian GEM model outrank the GFS blend for same-day calls, and the fifteen-minute nowcast is not radar.

Moose on Highway 60, and this is not a footnote

Collision risk peaks at dawn and dusk, exactly when you would be driving for a first-light paddle. The risk of hitting a deer in the hour after sunset runs around thirty times daylight levels, collisions cluster in the two hours after sunset, and most moose collisions happen after dark. A moose is roughly a tonne of animal sitting above the headlight beam with legs the height of a windscreen. This is not a deer-sized problem.

The stretches that matter are the wetland ground between the West Gate and Lake of Two Rivers, and the unlit rural sections between Huntsville and Dwight. Sunset that week is 19:59 to 20:12, so slow down materially on any drive home from an evening paddle.
