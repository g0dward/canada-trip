Admin, and what it actually is

Nothing here is mandatory except by consequence of what you decide to do. Two of these are car park permits for provincial parks, one is a free form, and the paddle cannot be booked at all.

The Algonquin day permit

$21 per car, per day

This is a daily vehicle permit, not an entry ticket for people. One covers the car and everyone in it. It exists because the park manages car park capacity along the Highway 60 corridor, the 56 km strip of road that runs through the south of Algonquin with all the trailheads, beaches and the Visitor Centre on it.

You may genuinely never have bought one, and here is why. You do not need it to drive Highway 60 straight through without stopping. You do not need it for the Ragged Falls paddle, because Oxtongue Lake sits outside the park boundary and reaching the falls by water avoids the car park entirely. And Ontario Parks only moved to advance online day-use permits in the last few years, so on an older visit you would simply have paid at the gate on arrival.

You only need one if you want a day inside the park proper. On this page that means the Smoke Lake paddle, Sunday Creek, swimming at Lake of Two Rivers, or the Visitor Centre. If the trip stays on the Oxtongue River, Limberlost and the lakes around the house, you never buy one.

If you do want it: reservations.ontarioparks.ca, released exactly five days ahead at 07:00 Eastern, which is noon UK time. Five days, not five months. Pick "Algonquin, Hwy 60 Corridor" from the dropdown, because the park sells eight separate day-use areas and a permit for one is not valid at another. Enter the hire car registration. All sales are final after two hours, so do not book a weather-dependent day speculatively.

The Arrowhead day permit

$21 per car, per day

Arrowhead is the provincial park ten minutes north of Huntsville, off Highway 11. You have been before: Stubb's Falls is one of its trails and it is on your AllTrails list. Same kind of daily vehicle permit as Algonquin, and the reason it needs advance booking is that the gatehouse has been permanently closed and the park went paperless, so 100 per cent of permits are now sold online in advance.

Again, only needed if you go. The one walk here worth a morning is Arrowhead Lake plus Beaver Meadow, just under nine kilometres. If you skip that in favour of Limberlost, which is free, you never buy this either.

The risk is real if you do go. Arrowhead fills on summer weekends, and arriving without a permit on a full day means being turned away at the entrance. Same booking window as Algonquin: five days ahead, 07:00 Eastern. So if you want both, they happen in one sitting.

The Limberlost waiver

Free

Limberlost Forest and Wildlife Reserve is the ten thousand acre private nature reserve half an hour east of the house, with seventy kilometres of trail and twenty lakes, described in the walking section below. It is free to use and there is no booking and no permit.

What there is, is a safety waiver: a free online form the owners require every visitor to complete before arriving. It asks for names, contact details, an emergency contact and the vehicle registration, which is why it has to wait until you have the RAV4. Do it on the evening of the 22nd rather than at the gate.

Restaurant bookings

Only two are worth thinking about in advance. Sunnys Chinese takes OpenTable and a few days out is usually enough midweek, longer for a weekend. Bernhardt's takes Resy and is the one to book as early as you can, because demand is loud enough that reservations resell for around $132 on the secondary market. If you want Mimi Chinese, book the day its window opens.

I had Osteria Giulia in this list earlier and I was wrong to. It is a Michelin-listed Italian restaurant on Avenue Road, and it only made the list because its booking window is unusually tight, releasing tables exactly fourteen days ahead at midnight. But it is polished, uptown, and closer to the sort of evening you said you did not want. Ignore it unless the others fall through.

The paddle

Cannot be booked

Worth repeating here because it is the one thing you actually care about. No reservations, first come first served on a finite fleet of boats. There is nothing to lock in, so the whole plan is choosing a weekday and being at the door when it opens. The only outstanding question is what time that is, which is the phone call.

The islands, by kayak

Rent on the island, not at Harbourfront

This is the decision that makes or breaks the day. Toronto Island SUP launches from the Algonquin Bridge on Ward's Island, which is the mouth of the lagoon network, so you are in the good water within a minute. Harbourfront at 283A Queens Quay W means paddling across the open harbour, over the ferry lanes and the Billy Bishop channel, twice, which eats most of a two hour rental before you reach anything worth seeing. Harbourfront is $50 solo or $70 tandem for two hours, and its one real advantage is that on a windy day it shuttles renters to sheltered island water instead.

The route: west from the Algonquin Bridge past the Queen City Yacht Club and the floating homes, through Sunfish Cut, under the footbridge into Turtle Alley, then out to Olympic Island. Sheltered, wooded, and nothing like the Centre Island crowds.

Manage one expectation. There are no otters here. Mink and beaver are the closest it gets. This is a paddle for the cottage community, the herons and the quiet, not for the wildlife day you had up north.

Getting there, and eating after

Take the Ward's Island ferry rather than Centre Island. Two corrections to what most guides still say: the Rectory Café no longer exists, and 102 Lakeshore Ave is now The Riviera. The better stop is the Island Café, whose clubhouse burned down in March 2024 and reopened fully in June 2025, which makes it both the better story and the better room.

Expect worse queues than normal. Youth and seniors ride the island ferry free for the whole of August 2026. Go early. Toronto Island SUP's 2026 kayak prices are hidden behind images on their site, so ring ahead rather than turn up guessing.

Takeaway and delivery

How it works here

The apps are Uber Eats, DoorDash and SkipTheDishes, the last being the Canadian one and usually the widest in a neighbourhood like this. Expect delivery fees plus a service fee plus a tip prompt that starts high; tipping around 15 per cent is normal and the default suggestion will be more.

Better than the apps, and closer: Bairradino does the whole thing to take out and has a takeaway-only second counter at 222 Lansdowne. A whole charcoal chicken, potatoes and rice carried three minutes up the road beats anything that arrives lukewarm in a bag. Juniors will do you a pizza late.

This is the thinnest section on the page. Delivery coverage was not researched properly, so treat the app names as orientation rather than a recommendation, and check what actually delivers to Lansdowne on the night.
Ring before you walk anywhere in the second half of August. This is exactly when Toronto's Portuguese and Italian family businesses shut for two weeks with nothing but a sign in the window. Baldassarre is the proof, and it took its retail counter with it on the 14th.

Water

The fixed point: Oxtongue River to Ragged Falls

The paddle from the last trip, the one with the otters. Three to four hours return, out across Oxtongue Lake then up the river, landing at the base of the falls where you can scramble the rock and swim. Late August low water means the falls are less dramatic than in spring but far more of the rock is open. 20 to 25 minutes from the door.

There is nothing to reserve, and that is the thing to plan around. It is first come first served on a finite fleet of boats, so the day is won or lost by being there when the doors open. Three things follow from that:

Ask for the "Ragged Falls Paddling Experience" by name. The named day-trip rate is cheaper than the generic same-day rate for the identical boat: $49.98 for a tandem canoe against $53.98, or $46.98 for a single kayak. Paddles, buoyancy aids and safety kits are all included. About $56 with tax.

Pick a weekday and go at opening. The falls are quiet first thing and busy by midday, when the separate Highway 60 car park crowd arrives on foot. Oxtongue Lake also picks up wind through the afternoon, and the lake leg is the exposed part. Avoid Saturday the 23rd and Friday the 28th.

No park permit needed. The store is outside Algonquin, and arriving at the falls by water means you never touch the pay-and-display car park. This is the one good day up here that costs nothing but the boat.

The one call that matters

Ring Algonquin Outfitters on 705-635-2243 before you fly. Their 2026 opening hour is genuinely unclear: sources split between 8am and 9am, and their Canoe Lake store publishes 9am to 6pm as the closest proxy. That single hour is the difference between having the falls to yourselves and sharing them, and it is the only unknown left on the fixed point of the trip.

Worth asking in the same call: whether a same-day boat can go off-site, and whether they hire tie-downs. The RAV4 will not have roof racks, and the answer decides whether the Lower Oxtongue paddle below is available to you at all.

The find: the Lower Oxtongue to Marsh's Falls

Launch from Dwight Beach, paddle a kilometre west along the bay to the river mouth, then four and a half kilometres up a meandering wetland river to Marsh's Falls. 8.8 km return, about three hours at an exploring pace, free, no permit, no gate. Dwight is 15 to 18 minutes from the door and on the way to Oxtongue Lake.

This is a wildlife corridor rather than a scenic lake, which is the difference. Herons, beaver, otter, turtles and moose are all on the expected list, and the river runs through the low wet outwash ground that moose actually use. The Lake of Bays Heritage Foundation runs an annual eco-tour on exactly this route and publishes a free downloadable paddle guide, so get that before you fly.

One catch, worth a phone call. There is no rental at Dwight Beach, and the RAV4 will not have roof racks. Ask Algonquin Outfitters directly whether a same-day rental can go off-site and whether they will hire you tie-downs. If yes, this is the best paddle of the week.

No driving at all

The canal outside the door

Zero minutes

The historic Portage Canal joins Fairy Lake to Peninsula Lake and is navigable. At midday it is a working powerboat route through a resort town and not worth paddling. At 06:30 it is glass. Worth doing once for the novelty of stepping out of the door onto water.

Skip Canoe Lake, and the one that got away

Canoe Lake is the most famous launch in Algonquin and also the busiest. It is an access point, an outfitter, a restaurant and a permit office on one bay. For someone who has been to Algonquin twice, the Tom Thomson pilgrimage is a queue with a cairn at the end. Smoke Lake is 600 metres further west on the same road and is a better paddle. Skip Cache Lake and Tea Lake entirely.

The Big East River is the best paddle you probably cannot do. A 17 km run past the Arrowhead side, sandy-bottomed and quiet, but it is one way and needs two cars. With one RAV4 and no documented shuttle service, it does not work. Worth ten minutes ringing a Huntsville taxi, because if one will run the shuttle this beats everything except the Lower Oxtongue.

Walking

The standout

Limberlost Forest and Wildlife Reserve

1002 Buck Lake Landing, 30 minMap

Ten thousand acres, seventy kilometres of trail, twenty lakes, free, and no marketing budget. The only place here that gives you a genuine half day on the hill rather than a car park and a viewpoint. Reviewers keep describing it as what you do after you see the queue at Arrowhead.

The one to do is Solitaire plus Buck Lake plus the Buck Lake Lookout, about 12 km over four hours, two contrasting lakes and a short steep sting at the end that gives the day a summit. For quieter still, start at the Barlow lot and take the Kalonga wetland boardwalks.

Two catches. A safety waiver is mandatory, has to be done online before you arrive, and asks for the vehicle registration. And the 09:00 to 17:00 gate hours are enforced, with vehicles ticketed or removed, so no dawn starts. Ignore the old master trail guide still linked from their own site, it lists Solitaire at 13 km when it is 6.3.

Arrowhead

10 minutes north of HuntsvilleMap

You have done Stubb's Falls. The only route here that makes a proper morning is Arrowhead Lake plus Beaver Meadow, just under nine kilometres, which Ontario Parks themselves recommend as a pair. Round the lake through wetland edges where herons work the shallows, then over Porcupine Bluffs past a beaver pond. Everything else is a half-hour stroll. Big Bend Lookout is a genuinely good view over a gooseneck in the Big East River but it is fifty metres from the car park, so treat it as a stop. Skip Homesteaders, it is half ATV track.

Ragged Falls on foot, and Lions Lookout

Ragged Falls is worth three quarters of an hour and not a day: under a kilometre of trail with a steep rough spur to the base. Bolt it onto an Algonquin day, since a corridor permit also covers its car park, or reach it by water. Lions Lookout in Huntsville is not a hike at all, there is a paved road to the top, but the view over Fairy Lake and the town is genuinely good, so drive up in the evening with a beer.

There is no Buttermilk Falls near Dwight. The one people mean is in Haliburton County, an hour and ten each way, for a two-minute walk. Only worth it if you are already going to the Dorset tower.

Moose on Highway 60, and this is not a footnote

Collision risk peaks at dawn and dusk, exactly when you would be driving for a first-light paddle. The risk of hitting a deer in the hour after sunset runs around thirty times daylight levels, collisions cluster in the two hours after sunset, and most moose collisions happen after dark. A moose is roughly a tonne of animal sitting above the headlight beam with legs the height of a windscreen. This is not a deer-sized problem.

The stretches that matter are the wetland ground between the West Gate and Lake of Two Rivers, and the unlit rural sections between Huntsville and Dwight. Sunset that week is 19:59 to 20:12, so slow down materially on any drive home from an evening paddle.

The Portage Flyer, and what to skip

The steam train is worth ten dollars, Tuesday to Saturday, tickets on the day only. Skip the pioneer village attached to it, and skip Santa's Village entirely, which is a fifty dollar a head toddler theme park.

The SS Bigwin is not sailing at all in 2026. The 1910 steamship that runs from Dwight Beach, ten minutes from your door, is ashore for repairs for the whole season. Third-party listings are still advertising it and the Cruise and Dine to Port Cunnington. Both are off.

The last morning, solved

You have to leave by about 13:00 on the 29th and there is nothing to do at that hour. Except that the Rosseau Fall Fair runs its 151st edition on Saturday 29 August, 10:00 to 16:00, and it sits roughly on the road to Pearson. Leave at 09:30, spend an hour and a half at a genuine country fair, and carry on to the airport. Far better than killing time in Huntsville.
