Five dates that change the plan

Toronto's turnover in 2026 has been brutal and most guides have not caught up. These are the ones that land inside your week.

  • Fri 14 Aug Famiglia Baldassarre serves its last public lunch, the day before you land. The Geary pasta shop closes for its usual Ferragosto break and does not reopen. Retail counter goes too. It survives only as a wholesale producer. Delete it from every list you have read. You can still eat the pasta at Bar Romy, 295 Adelaide St W.
  • Sat 15 Aug House of Smash, a smash burger stall, sets up outside Division 88 at 225 Geary Ave. Also on the 16th and the 22nd. Cash in hand, industrial street, no frontage. Arrival day, eight minutes' walk.
  • Sun 16 Aug Three things at once. The Geary Avenue Takeover, free, from 15:00 at 226 Geary. The Electric Bill Bar's final night, 866 Bloor St W, an Australian cocktail bar from the Civil Liberties team closing after two years because the city "isn't viable", so order a Big Koala. And MOCA's current season ends, which matters because it is a twelve minute walk away and then shut until October. Also the only Blue Jays home game of your week, 13:37 against the Yankees.
  • Fri 21 Aug The CNE opens. A genuine local institution, and note it is the 21st, not the 14th as one widely-ranked site claims.
  • Fri 21 to Sun 23 Toronto Folk Festival on Ward's Island, in the rebuilt clubhouse. The single best thing on in your week, and it overlaps your last two days in the city.
  • Sat 22 Aug A gig at Burdock Music Hall, 1184 Bloor St W, on your last night before driving north.
  • Already gone Big Trouble Pizza closed in January, Dark Horse Geary in June, and Indie Alehouse, Honest Weight, Vesuvio, Farmhouse Tavern, Superpoint and Hopgood's are all shut. Momofuku went in 2024. The Festival of South Asia moved to July, so ignore anything that says August.

The big dinner

Momofuku was loud, expensive, not remotely reverent, and had a point of view. The nearest thing Toronto has now is a pair of restaurants from the same group.

First choice

Parallel, 217 Geary Ave is a working tahini mill with a Middle Eastern restaurant attached. The stone mill is in the room and the tahini is the entire point. The islands, by kayak

Rent on the island, not at Harbourfront

This is the decision that makes or breaks the day. Toronto Island SUP launches from the Algonquin Bridge on Ward's Island, which is the mouth of the lagoon network, so you are in the good water within a minute. Harbourfront at 283A Queens Quay W means paddling across the open harbour, over the ferry lanes and the Billy Bishop channel, twice, which eats most of a two hour rental before you reach anything worth seeing. Harbourfront is $50 solo or $70 tandem for two hours, and its one real advantage is that on a windy day it shuttles renters to sheltered island water instead.

The route: west from the Algonquin Bridge past the Queen City Yacht Club and the floating homes, through Sunfish Cut, under the footbridge into Turtle Alley, then out to Olympic Island. Sheltered, wooded, and nothing like the Centre Island crowds.

Manage one expectation. There are no otters here. Mink and beaver are the closest it gets. This is a paddle for the cottage community, the herons and the quiet, not for the wildlife day you had up north.

Getting there, and eating after

Take the Ward's Island ferry rather than Centre Island. Two corrections to what most guides still say: the Rectory Café no longer exists, and 102 Lakeshore Ave is now The Riviera. The better stop is the Island Café, whose clubhouse burned down in March 2024 and reopened fully in June 2025, which makes it both the better story and the better room.

Expect worse queues than normal. Youth and seniors ride the island ferry free for the whole of August 2026. Go early. Toronto Island SUP's 2026 kayak prices are hidden behind images on their site, so ring ahead rather than turn up guessing.

Nearest branch is 106 John St, downtown

What the filter cost, and one verdict reversed

The most-named place in the whole sweep fails the filter by 249 reviews. Thai Nyyom, 1419 Bloor W, twelve minutes away, named in eight separate threads, 4.8 from 749. If you override the rule once, this is the one. Reddit's khao soi consensus lives here now, not at Pai.

Which is the reversal: skip the Pai queue. Reddit spent two years pushing back on it, including a whole thread titled "Is Pai still the best Thai in Toronto" that answers no. The friend recommendation stands on the page above, but the crowd that queues there is the story now.

And one death notice: The Fall Bright Tavern, 840 Bloor W, had three threads and a Toronto Life nod behind it, and its own homepage now reads "We've sold our little restaurant." Whatever replaces it is unknown.

Permanently closed: PLANTA Queen West, Vinoteca Pompette, La Cubana Roncesvalles (Proper took the space, and it is on this page), and Pat's Homestyle Jamaican. Temporarily closed: Superpoint and Kibo Secret Garden. Google already marks them, but a saved list makes it easy to tap an old pin without noticing.

Also a timing miss from the list's own notes: Kensington's pedestrian Sunday is the last Sunday of the month, 30 August. You will be in Algonquin.

Cubano Kings

141 Spadina Ave, takeout windowMap

An 18-square-foot Cuban kitchen behind a takeout window, doing pressed cubanos, pastelitos and Cuban coffee for under $10. Downtown, so it is the cheap lunch on a Kensington or Distillery day rather than a trip of its own. The Pan con Timba, Swiss cheese and guava on buttery bread, is the order nobody regrets.

Two more, with caveats

Tatsuro's, 1378 Queen St E Map is a Japanese-Italian brunch diner on housemade milk bread, 4.8 and walk-in only, but it is in Leslieville, a long way east; pair it with a Tommy Thompson or Riverdale day or let it go. Conejo Negro, 838 College St Map is the one worth breaking the review rule for: it is over the line at 987, but it is a Michelin Bib Gourmand doing Caribbean and Creole, it was the list's starred "hipster soul food" pick, and College is fifteen minutes away. Takeaway and delivery

How it works here

The apps are Uber Eats, DoorDash and SkipTheDishes, the last being the Canadian one and usually the widest in a neighbourhood like this. Expect delivery fees plus a service fee plus a tip prompt that starts high; tipping around 15 per cent is normal and the default suggestion will be more.

Better than the apps, and closer: Bairradino does the whole thing to take out and has a takeaway-only second counter at 222 Lansdowne. A whole charcoal chicken, potatoes and rice carried three minutes up the road beats anything that arrives lukewarm in a bag. Juniors will do you a pizza late.

This is the thinnest section on the page. Delivery coverage was not researched properly, so treat the app names as orientation rather than a recommendation, and check what actually delivers to Lansdowne on the night.
Ring before you walk anywhere in the second half of August. This is exactly when Toronto's Portuguese and Italian family businesses shut for two weeks with nothing but a sign in the window. Baldassarre is the proof, and it took its retail counter with it on the 14th.

Clothes

The honest version first: orSlow, nanamica and Auralee all have UK stockists now, so Canada is not a magic unlock. What it is: orSlow basics run roughly a third cheaper in CAD (the 105 one-wash is $379, about £205, against £320 at Care of Carl), mid-August is sale season and every shop below has one running, and two of the tee briefs get solved outright. Buy where the sticker is genuinely better, browse the rest. All verified open August 2026; prices in CAD, sterling at about 1.85.

The walks

Written out properly, in the same spirit as the Barbican tour: architecture and streets as social and political story rather than a list of pretty buildings. Two start at the front door, the rest are a subway ride. Each has its own page with stops, quotes and the honest traps.

From the door · 4.4 km

Three railways and a chocolate factory

The Junction Triangle

Rail on all three sides, a footbridge from 1907 that was meant to be temporary, the working Nestlé plant that never left, and the aluminium foundry that became MOCA. Do this on Sunday 16 August and end at MOCA's doors for 10:00, its last day before it shuts until October.
Walk it →

Two stops away · an evening

Ninety-seven years without a drink

The Junction

A man killed walking home from a hotel in 1897, a total ban from 1 May 1904, and the last dry district in Toronto until 2001. Four chartered banks on a street where you could not buy a drink, one of them built for the Molson family. Ends by crossing the line where the ban broke, into a pint.
Walk it →

Downtown · 2.6 km

Who is the city for?

Distillery District to St. Lawrence

The Barbican and Golden Lane walk, transplanted. The best-preserved Victorian industrial site on the continent, now selling a designed three-hour experience, against North America's most successful piece of social housing, which nobody visits because there is nothing to visit. A weekday, starting 10:30, before the country festival starts at one.
Walk it →

Six stops away · 3.5 km and a climb

A bit of England far from England

The Humber, the portage that is the reason Toronto exists, and the Seneca village of Teiaiagon under an Edwardian enclave sold in Latin. The deepest of the four and the one that needs handling with care: two Seneca women were found buried under these gardens in 1999 and 2006.
Walk it →

Downtown · 4.6 km · a weekday

Their ways are not ours

The Ward to Kensington

The migration walk, for Devni. Toronto's first arrival neighbourhood was branded a slum in 1911 and demolished for City Hall; its Chinatown, Little Italy and Jewish market scattered west, and the walk follows them: from the erasure, through the Chinatown Jean Lumb saved, into Kensington read as strata. Ends on a dive patio, with a Little Italy dinner extension.

Chinatown to Ossington · 3 km · go hungry

Eat the layers

The snack-crawl version

The Arrival City walk rebuilt as food: one pastry per diaspora. A Mashion egg tart in Chinatown, a Golden Patty and a churro in Kensington, a nata duel in Little Portugal, and a flat white or a Bellwoods pint on Ossington as the newest layer of all. Bring cash, start hungry.

Nobody else is running these

Heritage Toronto puts on sixty-odd free volunteer-led tours a season and it is worth a look at their events page nearer the time, but nothing in their listings falls in the west end, on the Humber or in St. Lawrence during your week. The one that would have been perfect, an archaeologist-led walk over Baby Point, runs on 9 August and again on 6 September. You miss it by six days. Ring them on 416-338-1338 and ask about a private booking, because ninety minutes with a field archaeologist beats anything written down.
